## 3. Diagnosis by Issue

### 🔴 Issue A: Checkpoint selected on val_loss — misses PnL peak (v3 most affected)

v3's val_loss is minimised at **epoch 2 (0.02381)** but its actual PnL peak is at **epoch 4 (889)**:

| v3 epoch | val_loss | PnL | Sell recall | Buy recall |
|----------|----------|-----|-------------|------------|
| 2 ← saved | 0.02381 | 872 | 0.379 | 0.328 |
| 4 | 0.02413 | **889** | 0.345 | 0.222 |

The saved checkpoint leaves 17 points of PnL on the table. This is a direct consequence of the checkpoint logic flagged in code-review-recommendations.md issue #5. When extended to 25+ epochs, this gap is likely to grow.

### 🔴 Issue B: v2 instability — se_context_window=64 is too large

v2's val_loss bottoms at epoch 1 (0.02479) and then oscillates without improvement. The larger SE context window (64 bars) relative to seq_len (256) means the SE block is aggregating over 25% of the input sequence — likely over-smoothing the temporal signal and creating a noisier loss gradient. Best epoch at 1 out of 7 run epochs is a red flag. **v2 should not be used.**

### 🟡 Issue C: val_loss oscillation across all configs — learning rate on the edge of stability

v3 val_loss sequence: `0.0284 → 0.0246 → 0.0238 → 0.0258 → 0.0241 → 0.0247 → 0.0245 → 0.0254`

There is no smooth downward trend — the loss bounces ±10% each epoch. This is a sign the learning rate (0.00015) is slightly too high for stable convergence, or the 80-strength recall floor constraint is contributing instability by firing inconsistently between epochs. v1 is better-behaved on this axis (val_loss roughly flat after epoch 2, best at epoch 7).

### 🟡 Issue D: Epoch budget too short

Train loss is still clearly declining in all configs at the final epoch:

| Config | Train loss epoch 0 | Train loss final | Final epoch |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|-------------|
| v1 | 14.16 | 9.48 | 10 |
| v2 | 14.13 | 9.79 | 7 |
| v3 | 13.87 | 9.67 | 8 |

None of the models have converged. The train loss has only dropped ~33% from its starting value. With 25–30 epochs the model would likely reach a meaningfully lower optimum, but val_loss oscillation needs to be addressed first (see Change 2 below) or the checkpoint will still be unstable.

### 🟡 Issue E: Production models use val_bars=10000 — far too small for reliable checkpoint selection

The production runs (both April 9 and April 21) use `val_bars=10000`. Comparing against the sweep:

| Metric | Sweep v3 (196k val) | Prod Apr21 (10k val) |
|--------|---------------------|----------------------|
| PnL range across epochs | -79 → +889 | 12 → 105 |
| Best F1 sell | 0.361 | 0.337 |
| Best F1 buy | 0.340 | 0.367 |
| Val loss scale | 0.023–0.028 | 8.45–11.0 |
| Best epoch | 2 | 13 |
| Recall buy (range) | 0.14–0.35 | 0.02–0.30 |

The 10k bar val set produces extreme noise: precision_sell reaches 0.769 at epoch 11 (impossible to sustain at scale) while recall_buy collapses to 0.04 at epoch 16. Checkpoint selection on this signal picks the epoch with the best noise artefact, not the best model. The sweep's 196k val set gives stable, representative metrics. **Production training should use the same 20% holdout approach as the sweeps.**

### 🟡 Issue F: BUY consistently underperforms SELL

Across all configs and epochs, recall_buy < recall_sell by 5–10 percentage points. At v3's best epoch: recall_sell=0.379 vs recall_buy=0.328. The asymmetry likely reflects a labelling characteristic of US500 — in a long-term uptrending index, pullbacks that form valid SELL setups are structurally cleaner than BUY breakouts (which often occur in noisy momentum bursts). Two possible mitigations: (1) lower the `trade_bias_offset` for SELL to reduce its signal count and even the balance, or (2) add a per-class bias init offset per direction.

### 🟢 Issue G: Class weights give FLAT 3.5× more weight than SELL/BUY

Stored weights: `[SELL=0.545, FLAT=1.909, BUY=0.545]`. For a ~96% FLAT dataset, this inverts the standard inverse-frequency prescription (which would give SELL/BUY ~16× the weight of FLAT). This is clearly an intentional precision-first tuning decision (make the model conservative), backed up by the recall floor preventing total recall collapse. It is working — precision on SELL/BUY sits at 0.34–0.38 and PnL is positive. This is not a bug to fix, but worth noting as a design choice.

### 🟢 Issue H: commission=0 across all runs

Every sweep and production run uses `commission=0.0`. US500 typical round-trip spread is ~0.3–0.5 index points. With avg_pnl_per_trade around 0.097–0.170 in the sweep runs (raw units), commission will meaningfully erode real-world PnL. The model will be overtrained toward marginal trades that are profitable pre-commission but not after.

## 5. Code Change Plan

### Change 1: Checkpoint on best PnL, not best val_loss
**Priority:** High  
**File:** `ModelWorkbench/train_prod_model.py` (lines 586–592 per code-review-recommendations.md #5)  
**Why:** v3's val_loss-selected checkpoint (epoch 2, PnL=872) misses the PnL peak at epoch 4 (PnL=889). With 30 epochs this gap will grow further.  
**What to change:** Save checkpoint when `eval_pack["profit"] > history.get("best_profit", float("-inf"))`. Also add `"best_profit": float("-inf")` to the history dict at initialisation. (Full implementation in code-review-recommendations.md #5.)  
**Expected impact:** Recover the PnL peak epoch. For v3 this is +17 PnL points at 10 epochs; at 30 epochs the gain is likely larger.

### Change 2: Reduce learning rate from 0.00015 → 0.00010 in the US500 launcher
**Priority:** High  
**File:** `Engine/.run_US500.py` training launcher (or the hidden `.train_US500_LSTM.py` script)  
**Why:** Val_loss oscillates ±10% each epoch in all three sweep configs. v2's val_loss never recovers after epoch 1. A 33% LR reduction should smooth the loss surface without significantly slowing convergence over 30 epochs.  
**Expected impact:** Smoother val_loss curve, more reliable best-epoch selection, and reduced chance of the loss diverging as in v2.

### Change 3: Use 20% holdout for production training (not val_bars=10000)
**Priority:** High  
**File:** Hidden `.train_US500_LSTM.py` launcher — change `val_bars=10000` to `holdout_size=0.20`  
**Why:** The 10k-bar val set produces oscillating, unrepresentative metrics (precision_sell hit 0.769 in the prod run — an artefact of tiny sample size). The sweep's 196k val set gave stable F1 and PnL curves. Checkpoint selection on 10k bars is unreliable.  
**Expected impact:** Val metrics will stabilise, making checkpoint selection meaningful. Expected F1 sell ≥ 0.33, F1 buy ≥ 0.27 at best epoch (consistent with sweep v3).

### Change 4: Extend epoch budget to 30 in the US500 launcher
**Priority:** Medium  
**File:** Hidden `.train_US500_LSTM.py`  
**Why:** Train loss at epoch 10 is still 9.67 (started 13.87) — only 33% of the way to convergence. With a stable val set and the LR reduction from Change 2, 30 epochs should allow the model to converge and val_loss to settle.  
**Expected impact:** Best-epoch PnL should exceed the sweep's 872–889. Rough extrapolation from the train loss curve suggests convergence around 8.0–8.5, implying 20+ epochs of useful training remain.

### Change 5: Reduce rec_floor_weight from 80 → 60
**Priority:** Medium  
**File:** Hidden `.train_US500_LSTM.py` loss params  
**Why:** The strong recall floor weight (80) fires a quadratic penalty any epoch where recall drops below 0.25. This penalty magnitude can dominate the loss and cause the oscillation observed in val_loss. At 60, the floor is still enforced but with less gradient shock.  
**Expected impact:** Smoother training dynamics, fewer loss spikes. Trade recall may drift slightly lower in some epochs but should stay above the 0.25 floor.

### Change 6: Add realistic commission to training
**Priority:** Medium  
**File:** Hidden `.train_US500_LSTM.py`  
**Why:** All current runs use `commission=0.0`. US500 round-trip cost is ~0.3–0.5 index points. With avg_pnl_per_trade ~0.10–0.17, commission could eliminate marginal trades. Training with `commission=0.4` will cause the model to learn to avoid marginal signals, improving live performance.  
**Expected impact:** Lower raw PnL on validation (PnL metric drops) but higher avg_pnl_per_trade and better live profitability. Also ties in with Change 1 (PnL checkpoint) to ensure commission is reflected in checkpoint selection.

### Change 7: Set se_context_window=32 (reject se_context_window=64)
**Priority:** Low  
**File:** Hidden `.train_US500_LSTM.py` architecture params  
**Why:** v2 used se_context_window=64 and showed worst performance (best epoch at 1, val_loss oscillating, no recovery). v1 and v3 both use 32 and significantly outperform it. The SE window of 64 represents 25% of the sequence (256 bars) — aggregating too much temporal context into the channel attention gate.  
**Expected impact:** Avoids the instability pattern seen in v2. Keep se_context_window=32.
